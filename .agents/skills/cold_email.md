# Cold Email Copywriting & Outreach Skill

This skill provides a systematic framework for cold email copywriting based on the 4-step framework and offer formula, used to secure B2B clients, partnerships, and sponsorships. Follow these rules when generating cold outbound campaigns.

## The 4-Step Copyrighting Framework

Every outbound message (email, LinkedIn DM, SMS) must contain these 4 components in order:

### 1. Personalization
- **Goal:** Defeat the "is this a spammer?" objection and buy 30 seconds of attention.
- **Rule:** Keep it short (1-2 sentences max) and informal. Avoid corporate speak ("Hope this finds you well").
- **Technique (Cold Reading):** Make broad statements that seem highly specific but apply to ~80% of the target group.
- **Example:** "Hey Nick, love your channel man. Very no BS and has helped me get started in management consulting. I think I can help you with something and return the favor."

### 2. Who Am I and Why Do I Matter? (Identity & Social Proof)
- **Goal:** Establish authority and in-group alignment. 
- **Rule:** 1-2 sentences summarizing who you are, supported by quantified social proof. Borrow credibility if needed.
- **Example:** "I currently work with [Client Name/Type], a company around your size, to help them [Action]. We generated [X result] in the last [Timeframe]."
- *Note:* Match the reference group. If emailing a B2B SaaS, mention a B2B SaaS case study.

### 3. The Offer
- **Goal:** Make an offer so good that saying "no" feels irrational.
- **Formula:** `I will do [X outcome] in [Y time] or [Z risk mitigation/guarantee].`
- **Rule:** Point out an observation/pain point, then deliver the quantified, time-bound, risk-free offer. 
- **Example:** "I went through your landing page and you're bleeding money. I'm so confident I can fix this, I bet I could generate at least 100k for you in the next 60 days exactly or you won't pay a cent."
- **Conversion Math:** `CVR = (ROI * Trust) / Friction`. Maximize ROI & Trust, minimize Friction.

### 4. Call To Action (CTA)
- **Goal:** Get them to take ONE effortless step.
- **Rule:** Do not be vague ("Let me know your thoughts"). Provide an exact action or time.
- **Example:** "Would you be open to a 15-minute call? If so, I can give you a ring at 3:30 PM today or before 12 PM tomorrow. Just say yes and I'll send a one-click invite."

## Framing & Psychology Principles
- **Write like a human:** It must pass the "text message test" (looks like a personal message from a friend, not a mass campaign). Kill corporate signals. Use lowercase for specific demographics if appropriate. Introduce a minor typo at the end intentionally to show humanization if sending mass.
- **Give First:** Offer free upfront value (audit, 500-word post, mockups) to invoke reciprocity.
- **Micro Commitments:** Keep the first ask incredibly low friction (e.g. "Just reply yes", "Watch this 30s video") before escalating to bigger asks.

## Common Offer Templates
1. **Lead Gen:** "I will guarantee you [X] booked sales appointments in the next [Y] days or you don't pay. Just say yes and I'll get started."
2. **Product Delivery:** "I will build you [Asset] at no cost. Only if you absolutely love it will I ask you to work with me. Send me [Input] and I'll return it in 48 hours."
3. **Self-Liquidating / Free Initial:** "I will rewrite your last 3 email campaigns for free. Plus, I'll show you why the new versions will convert better. No strings."

## Iteration
- Do not expect a new template to crush it the first try.
- Send batches (e.g., 500 emails). Measure Opens -> Replies -> Bookings.
- Kill underperforming variants quickly, analyze top performers, and iterate. 
- *The system* survives longer than any specific template.
